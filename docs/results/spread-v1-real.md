# spread-v1 on real-connectome subgraphs: summary

Registered in `docs/metrics.md` ("spread-v1 on real-connectome subgraphs", committed
2026-10-26 before the run). Per-dataset reports, generated by `scripts/spread.py`:
`spread-v1-malecns@1.0.md`, `spread-v1-banc@888.md`, `spread-v1-flywire@783.md`.
Level A, 10 seeds × 2 sides × 4 variants per dataset, about 390 s wall each.

## Pre-registered result

Wiring effect, paired E_real − E_null over seeds (degrees of turn-away, 95 % bootstrap CI):

| dataset | subgraph (neurons / edges) | real − rewired | real − sign_shuffled |
|---|---|---|---|
| malecns@1.0 | 2721 / 100596 | +0.03 (−0.31 to 0.34), no evidence | +0.01 (−0.31 to 0.28), no evidence |
| banc@888 | 803 / 6308 | +0.27 (0.02 to 0.51), **real > null** | +0.17 (−0.03 to 0.34), no evidence |
| flywire@783 | 1539 / 29962 | +0.08 (−0.23 to 0.39), no evidence | −0.10 (−0.24 to 0.04), no evidence |

The registered prediction was "no evidence anywhere". One of six comparisons crosses the
claim line.

## Why the BANC result is not a steering finding (exploratory, not registered)

- **Multiplicity.** The registration set no correction for 3 datasets × 2 nulls. With a
  Bonferroni level (99.17 % CI) the BANC interval is −0.06 to 0.60 and includes 0.
- **Size.** +0.27° is about 3 % of the controller's seed-to-seed drift SD (9°).
- **Mechanism.** The real BANC brain's turn readouts (DNa02) average 0.000 over the
  episode, and `scripts/dn_direction.py` shows a 0.001 readout moves heading by only
  about 0.1°. What does respond is the escape readout (DNp01), and unevenly: 0.005 for a
  loom on the left, 0.012 on the right. Escape slows both legs, so the two sides of an
  episode slow by different amounts and the controller's drift no longer cancels when the
  sides are averaged. Consistent with this, the per-seed BANC real − rewired difference
  correlates with that seed's no_brain drift (Pearson r = 0.64, p = 0.045; malecns r = 0.15,
  flywire r = −0.24).

**Reading:** with these frozen parameters the real looming subgraphs barely reach the
steering DNs. Their visible effect is through the giant fibre → slowing (secondary: in
malecns and flywire the real brain has the shortest path, 13.8 and 14.3 mm vs 15.0 mm
no_brain; in BANC, where the escape readout is weakest, rewired is shorter than real).
That fits the biology of the LPLC2/LC4 → giant fibre pathway, which is direct (one hop),
but at Level A it is a hand-written slowing, not escape.

## What this means for the next design

spread-v1 measures turning, and the real subgraphs do not drive turning. Two changes need
a new registered version (spread-v2), not a re-run of this one:

1. Remove the drift-escape coupling: measure turn-away against no_brain with the *same
   per-side slowing*, or use a speed-independent heading measure.
2. Decide the readout scale up front (for example, normalise each readout set by its
   maximum response to a reference stimulus) instead of using raw rates. Today the scale
   is set implicitly by the rate model's gain and by how much of each DN's input lies
   inside the subgraph.
